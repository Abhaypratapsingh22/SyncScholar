# Master Technical Prompt: Design an Intelligent PDF Processing, RAG-Based Knowledge Retrieval, and Grade-Aware AI Answering System for SyncScholar

## 1. Project Context and Objective

I am developing an AI-powered educational platform called **SyncScholar**, designed to help students understand academic concepts through an AI assistant that adapts its answers according to their selected educational level, syllabus, and expected knowledge.

I have provided:

- The extracted text containing the same chapter's content.

Your first task is to analyze and compare the original PDF with its extracted text. Identify differences, formatting problems, missing information, structural inconsistencies, and errors introduced during text extraction.

Based on this analysis, design a reusable AI-agent instruction system that can process educational PDFs of different subjects, classes, lengths, and formats.

The system must transform raw PDF content into structured, searchable, and semantically meaningful educational knowledge that can be stored in MongoDB and used by a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture.

**Important:** The textbook is not intended to be the exclusive source of answers. Its primary purpose is to provide educational context to the AI so that it understands the student's syllabus, expected vocabulary, conceptual depth, and level of understanding.

# 2. PDF Analysis and Intelligent Content Structuring

Develop detailed, general-purpose rules that instruct the PDF-processing agent to identify, interpret, clean, and structure educational content correctly.

### 2.1 Identify document hierarchy

The agent must correctly distinguish between:

- Book title
- Class and subject
- Chapter number and chapter title
- Main headings
- Subheadings
- Topics and subtopics
- Paragraphs
- Definitions
- Important facts
- Examples
- Activities and experiments
- Questions and exercises
- Tables and their contents
- Mathematical and scientific formulas
- Important notes and highlighted information
- References and supplementary content

The agent must understand the logical relationship between these elements instead of treating the entire extracted text as one continuous paragraph.

For example:

Chapter 1: Matter in Our Surroundings

1.1 Physical Nature of Matter

1.1.1 Matter is Made Up of Particles

The agent should recognize the chapter, topic, and subtopic hierarchy and maintain these relationships in the final structured output.

### 2.2 Handle figures, images, and captions

The agent must identify figure references such as:

- Fig. 1
- Fig. 1.4
- Figure 1.2
- Fig. 1: Structure of an Atom

Do not store meaningless figure references as independent educational concepts.

However:
- Preserve useful explanations surrounding the figures.
- Preserve captions if they contain meaningful educational information.
- Do not remove text merely because it appears close to a figure.
- If a diagram contains important information that is not available in the extracted text, flag it as potentially missing visual information rather than inventing its contents.

### 2.3 Handle mathematical and scientific expressions

Accurately interpret and preserve:

- Mathematical equations
- Chemical equations
- Scientific formulas
- Subscripts and superscripts
- Greek symbols
- Units and measurements
- Fractions and powers
- Inequalities
- Special mathematical characters
- Chemical symbols and reactions

For example, preserve:

- H₂O instead of H2O when the original meaning requires a subscript.
- CO₂ instead of CO2.
- E = mc² instead of E = mc2.
- Mathematical expressions in valid LaTeX where appropriate.

Never modify a formula simply to make it easier to store.

Where possible, maintain both a human-readable representation and a machine-readable representation.

### 2.4 Correct PDF extraction problems

The agent must identify and resolve:

- Broken sentences caused by page boundaries.
- Unnecessary line breaks.
- Incorrect paragraph separation.
- Repeated headers and footers.
- Duplicate content.
- Incorrect reading order.
- Missing spaces between words.
- OCR errors.
- Unusual Unicode characters.
- Incorrectly extracted tables.
- Sentences split across multiple pages.

The agent must not combine unrelated paragraphs or modify the original meaning while cleaning the text.

### 2.5 Preserve educational meaning

The objective is not simply to clean text. The agent must understand the educational purpose of each section.

For example, distinguish between:

- A definition explaining a concept.
- An example demonstrating the concept.
- An activity asking students to perform an experiment.
- A conclusion explaining the result.
- An exercise testing the student's understanding.

These elements must be represented separately in the structured knowledge base.

# 3. Intelligent Chunking Strategy for Large Educational PDFs

Implement a proper chunking strategy to divide large educational documents into smaller, meaningful sections.

A 500-page PDF should not be processed as one enormous text block every time a student asks a question.

Instead, the document must be processed once during ingestion and divided into smaller chunks.

### 3.1 What is chunking?

Chunking is the process of dividing a large document into smaller pieces of text called chunks.

For example:

A 500-page textbook may contain approximately 2,000 or more chunks, depending on its content and structure.

Each chunk should contain a meaningful educational section rather than an arbitrary collection of sentences.

### 3.2 Use structure-aware chunking

Do not divide the document exclusively according to a fixed number of characters.

Prefer a combination of:

1. Chapter-based segmentation.
2. Heading and subheading identification.
3. Paragraph-based segmentation.
4. Semantic segmentation.
5. Token-based chunk-size limits.

A chunk should ideally contain one complete concept, definition, explanation, example, or closely related group of paragraphs.

For example:

**Document:**

Class 9 Science → Chapter 1 → Matter in Our Surroundings → Physical Nature of Matter.

**Chunk 1:**
Introduction to matter.

**Chunk 2:**
Matter is made up of particles.

**Chunk 3:**
Characteristics of particles of matter.

**Chunk 4:**
Particles of matter have space between them.

**Chunk 5:**
Particles of matter are continuously moving.

This makes retrieval more meaningful than dividing the text after every arbitrary number of characters.

### 3.3 Chunk size and overlap

Develop an appropriate chunk-size strategy based on the educational content.

Use token-based chunk sizing rather than assuming that a fixed number of characters equals a fixed number of tokens.

Consider an initial configuration such as:

- Target chunk size: 400–700 tokens.
- Chunk overlap: 50–100 tokens.
- Maximum chunk size: configurable according to the embedding model and content type.

These values should be treated as starting points, not universal requirements.

Overlap is important because a concept may continue from one paragraph into another.

However, avoid excessive overlap because it creates duplicate information, increases storage requirements, and can affect retrieval quality.

### 3.4 Preserve contextual metadata in every chunk

Every chunk must retain enough information to identify its original location.

For example:

- Document ID
- Book name
- Class
- Subject
- Chapter number
- Chapter title
- Topic
- Subtopic
- Page number or page range
- Chunk ID
- Chunk sequence
- Content type
- Original source reference

Never store chunks as isolated text without their educational hierarchy.

# 4. Embedding Generation: Converting Text into Numerical Representations

After creating meaningful chunks, generate embeddings for each chunk.

### 4.1 What are embeddings?

Embeddings are numerical vector representations of text generated by an embedding model.

They represent semantic information about the text, allowing a system to identify content based on meaning rather than relying only on exact keyword matches.

For example:

Chunk A: "Matter is made up of very small particles."

Chunk B: "All substances consist of tiny particles."

Although the wording is different, both sentences communicate a similar concept.

An embedding model converts these texts into numerical vectors. Semantically similar texts generally have vectors that are close under the chosen similarity metric.

Do not interpret embeddings as simple keyword numbers. They are multidimensional numerical representations learned by an AI model.

### 4.2 Embedding generation workflow

During document ingestion:

1. Extract and clean the PDF text.
2. Divide the content into meaningful chunks.
3. Send each chunk to an embedding model.
4. Receive its numerical vector representation.
5. Associate the vector with the original chunk and its metadata.
6. Store the chunk, embedding, and metadata in MongoDB.

Example conceptual representation:

```json
{
  "chunk_id": "class9_science_ch1_chunk_001",
  "content": "Matter is made up of very small particles.",
  "embedding": [0.124, -0.342, 0.567, 0.891],
  "metadata": {
    "class": 9,
    "subject": "Science",
    "chapter": 1,
    "topic": "Matter in Our Surroundings",
    "subtopic": "Physical Nature of Matter",
    "page_number": 2,
    "content_type": "concept"
  }
}
```

The numerical vector above is only an illustrative example. Actual embedding dimensions and values depend on the selected model.

### 4.3 Important embedding rules

The agent and system architecture must ensure:

- Use a suitable embedding model for educational content.
- Use the same compatible embedding model for document chunks and student queries.
- Maintain consistent embedding dimensions.
- Never generate embeddings for empty or meaningless chunks.
- Avoid unnecessary embedding regeneration when document content has not changed.
- Track embedding model versions.
- Re-embed documents when changing to an incompatible embedding model.
- Batch embedding requests where supported to reduce processing time and API overhead.

Embedding generation should happen during document ingestion, not repeatedly whenever a student submits a question.

# 6. Complete RAG Architecture: Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Implement a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline for answering student questions.

Explain and implement the three main stages of RAG:

## R — Retrieval

Retrieval means finding the most relevant information from the stored educational knowledge base.

When a student submits a question, the system should not read the entire textbook again.

Instead, it should retrieve only the relevant chunks.

**Retrieval workflow:**

1. Receive the student's question.
2. Identify the selected class, subject, and other available context.
3. Convert the question into an embedding using the same compatible embedding model used during ingestion.
4. Perform vector similarity search in MongoDB.
5. Apply metadata filters based on the student's selected class, subject, board, and relevant document constraints.
6. Retrieve the most relevant chunks based on similarity.

For example:

Student question:

"What might happen to coastal cities in India if glaciers and polar ice keep melting faster?"

The system converts this question into a vector and searches for semantically related educational content.

It may retrieve chunks discussing:

- Melting glaciers.
- Rising sea levels.
- Flooding in coastal regions.
- Effects of climate change.
- Environmental consequences.

These chunks do not necessarily need to contain the exact wording of the student's question.

The purpose of vector search is to retrieve conceptually relevant information.

### Top-K retrieval

Explain how the system retrieves the top-K most relevant chunks.

For example:

- Retrieve the top 5 chunks.
- Evaluate their relevance.
- Optionally rerank them.
- Select the most useful 3–5 chunks.
- Pass only the necessary context to Gemini.

Do not blindly retrieve a large number of chunks. More retrieved content does not always mean better answers.

The retrieval count should be configurable according to question complexity and context size.

### Hybrid search

Consider implementing hybrid retrieval using:

- Vector search for semantic similarity.
- Full-text or keyword search for exact terminology.
- Metadata filtering for class, subject, and chapter.

This is especially useful when students ask questions containing specific scientific terms, chapter names, formulas, or definitions.

## A — Augmentation

Augmentation means combining the original student question with the relevant information retrieved from MongoDB.

The system should construct a contextual prompt containing:

1. Student question.
2. Selected class and subject.
3. Relevant syllabus information.
4. Retrieved textbook chunks.
5. Expected vocabulary level.
6. Expected conceptual depth.
7. Answer-generation instructions.

Example:

```text
Student Class: 9
Subject: Science

Question:
What might happen to coastal cities in India if glaciers
and polar ice keep melting faster?

Retrieved Educational Context:
1. Basic explanation of melting glaciers.
2. Introduction to rising sea levels.
3. Effects of flooding on coastal regions.

Instructions:
Explain the concept in simple language suitable
for a Class 9 student.

Avoid unnecessary advanced geographical models,
complex numerical projections, and university-level
scientific terminology.

Give a scientifically accurate and understandable answer.
```

The augmentation stage should ensure that Gemini receives sufficient educational context without sending the entire textbook.

Importantly, retrieved textbook content should guide the educational level, not restrict the answer to exact textbook sentences.

## G — Generation

Generation is the final stage in which Gemini uses the student's question and augmented educational context to generate the answer.

The Gemini API should generate responses that are:

- Scientifically accurate.
- Appropriate for the selected class.
- Easy to understand.
- Consistent with the student's syllabus.
- Written using suitable vocabulary.
- Detailed enough to explain the concept.
- Free from unnecessary advanced information.
- Relevant to the actual question.

The system should allow Gemini to use its broader knowledge when the retrieved textbook content does not directly cover a question.

However, the response must remain appropriate for the student's learning level.

# 7. Grade-Aware Knowledge Retrieval and Answer Generation

This is one of the most important features of SyncScholar.

The system must not generate the same explanation for students of every class.

### Example

Consider the question:

"What might happen to coastal cities in India if glaciers and polar ice keep melting faster?"

**If the student selects Class 9:**

Explain:
- Melting ice contributes to rising sea levels.
- Coastal areas may experience more flooding.
- People, buildings, roads, and farmland may be affected.
- Explain the concept using familiar vocabulary.

**If the student selects Class 12:**

The explanation may include:
- Thermal expansion of seawater.
- Contributions from land-based ice loss.
- Coastal vulnerability.
- Climate-related impacts.
- More detailed scientific terminology and mechanisms.

The underlying question is the same, but the expected explanation changes according to the student's educational level.

### Grade-level context parameters

Design a mechanism that retrieves or derives:

- Class level.
- Subject.
- Board and syllabus.
- Expected vocabulary.
- Conceptual complexity.
- Previously introduced concepts.
- Expected mathematical knowledge.
- Appropriate examples.
- Relevant learning objectives.
- Depth of explanation.

Store these parameters as structured metadata wherever practical.

Do not assume that the textbook contains every possible question or answer. The knowledge base should provide the educational boundaries and reference context.

# 9. Performance Optimization: 2–5 Second Response Target

A major system requirement is that a student should ideally receive an answer within **2–5 seconds** of submitting a question.

The system must be designed for low latency without compromising answer quality.

### 9.1 Separate ingestion and query processing

Implement two independent workflows.

**A. Offline document ingestion**

This happens when an administrator uploads a textbook.

1. PDF extraction.
2. Text cleaning.
3. Structural identification.
4. Content classification.
5. Semantic chunking.
6. Embedding generation.
7. Metadata assignment.
8. MongoDB storage.
9. Vector index availability verification.

This process may take minutes or longer depending on the document size. It must not happen during student question answering.

**B. Real-time question answering**

This happens whenever a student submits a question.

1. Receive the question and selected class.
2. Generate a query embedding.
3. Search MongoDB using vector and metadata filters.
4. Retrieve the top relevant chunks.
5. Construct the augmented prompt.
6. Call the Gemini API.
7. Return the generated answer.

This workflow should avoid unnecessary operations and retrieve only relevant information.

### 9.2 Latency optimization techniques

Consider:

- Efficient MongoDB vector indexes.
- Optimized database queries.
- Metadata pre-filtering.
- Embedding API batching during ingestion.
- Caching frequently requested educational context.
- Caching repeated query results where appropriate.
- Reusing persistent API connections.
- Keeping prompts compact.
- Avoiding unnecessary LLM calls.
- Avoiding multiple sequential retrieval stages unless needed.
- Streaming responses where appropriate.
- Selecting a Gemini model suitable for low-latency generation.

### 9.3 Realistic performance expectations

Do not assume that vector search automatically makes the complete response take two seconds.

Measure the latency of each stage separately:

| Processing stage | Target budget |
|---|---:|
| Request handling | 50–100 ms |
| Query embedding | 100–300 ms |
| MongoDB retrieval | 50–200 ms |
| Prompt construction | 20–50 ms |
| Gemini generation | 1–3 seconds |
| Network and remaining overhead | Variable |

These are initial engineering targets, not guaranteed timings.

The complete 2–5 second response objective must be validated through actual testing under realistic network conditions, concurrent users, and model/API latency.

Identify bottlenecks and optimize the most expensive stages.

SyncScholar should not behave like a traditional textbook search engine.

It should be capable of answering conceptual, practical, hypothetical, and application-based questions.

For example:

A Class 9 student asks:

"What might happen to coastal cities in India if glaciers and polar ice keep melting faster?"

The textbook might not directly discuss this exact question.

The system should:

1. Understand the intent of the question.
2. Retrieve related concepts from the Class 9 educational knowledge base.
3. Identify the student's expected knowledge level.
4. Use Gemini's broader knowledge to construct the explanation.
5. Avoid advanced details that are unnecessary for Class 9.
6. Deliver an accurate, understandable answer.

If no relevant textbook chunks are found, the system should not generate artificial textbook references or fabricate retrieved information.

It should use the available class-level and subject-level metadata, together with reliable general knowledge.

# 13. Important Instructions for the AI Agent

Follow these principles throughout the analysis and design process:

1. Do not treat PDF processing as simple text extraction. It requires structural understanding and semantic organization.
2. Do not assume that every PDF follows the same formatting or chapter structure.
3. Do not use arbitrary chunk boundaries when meaningful educational boundaries are available.
4. Do not generate embeddings repeatedly for unchanged document content.
5. Do not send entire textbooks to Gemini for every student question.
6. Do not confuse vector similarity with exact factual correctness.
7. Do not retrieve information without considering the student's selected class and subject.
8. Do not restrict Gemini to textbook wording when explaining concepts.
9. Do not introduce advanced concepts that exceed the student's expected learning level without a clear educational reason.
10. Do not compromise scientific accuracy for simpler explanations.
11. Do not fabricate missing information, formulas, figures, or textbook references.
12. Optimize for scalability, maintainability, retrieval accuracy, and low latency.
13. Prefer practical, production-oriented solutions over unnecessarily complicated architectures.
14. Explain the reasoning behind important technical decisions.
15. Clearly distinguish between ingestion-time operations and real-time question-answering operations.

**Final objective:**

Build SyncScholar as a scalable, RAG-powered, grade-aware educational AI assistant.

Its knowledge base should understand what a student is expected to know, while Gemini should use that context to explain concepts in an appropriate way.

The system should be capable of processing large educational PDFs, storing their knowledge efficiently, retrieving only the most relevant information, and generating accurate, syllabus-aligned answers within an ideal response time of 2–5 seconds.

The ultimate goal is not to make students memorize textbook passages. It is to help them understand any question at the right educational level using the textbook as a knowledge and learning reference.
